Approach

Every program earns its next step.

Drug discovery is a long sequence of decisions. We define the evidence a program needs before it moves forward, and we hold to it.

  1. Target validation

    We start with targets that have strong human evidence linking them to disease, then confirm the biology in cell systems that reflect the patient.

    → Validated target

  2. Hit finding

    We screen broadly and confirm carefully. Every hit is retested in dose response and checked in an orthogonal assay before chemistry begins.

    → Confirmed hit series

  3. Lead optimization

    Medicinal chemists improve potency, selectivity, and drug-like properties together, guided by structure and by measured data on every new analog.

    → Optimized lead

  4. Candidate selection

    A molecule becomes a development candidate when its efficacy, exposure, and safety data make a convincing case for the clinic.

    → Development candidate

Science

Most programs fail late. We work to find out early.

A single-concentration screen finds starting points. A full dose–response curve tells us whether a compound is worth pursuing. We measure the properties that end programs as early as we can, so our effort goes to molecules with a real chance in the clinic.

Potency
IC₅₀ and EC₅₀ in biochemical and cellular assays
Selectivity
Counter-screens against related targets and a broad safety panel
Exposure
ADME and pharmacokinetics from the earliest analogs
Safety
In vitro toxicity flags before any in vivo study

Regenerative medicine

Mesenchymal stem cells, measured like medicines.

Mesenchymal stem cells (MSCs) are adult stromal cells found in bone marrow, fat, and umbilical cord tissue. They can calm harmful inflammation and help damaged tissue recover, mostly through the signals they release. Our regenerative medicine program develops MSC therapies with the same standards of evidence we apply to drug discovery.

How MSCs work

Immune balance
MSCs damp overactive T-cell and macrophage responses through mediators such as PGE₂, IDO, and TGF-β.
Tissue repair
Growth factors and extracellular vesicles released by MSCs can support new blood vessel growth and help injured cells survive.
Differentiation
In culture, MSCs can become bone, cartilage, and fat cells. This is one of the defining tests of MSC identity.

Every lot we make is tested for identity, viability, sterility, and potency before it is used in a study.

Tri-lineage potential

Each lot is induced toward bone, fat, and cartilage, then stained to confirm the result. Shown here as illustrations of typical staining.

  • BoneAlizarin Red S · calcium deposits
  • FatOil Red O · lipid droplets
  • CartilageAlcian Blue · proteoglycan matrix

Investigational. Our MSC therapies are in development and have not been approved by the FDA or any other regulator. We do not provide them as treatments outside of authorized clinical studies.
